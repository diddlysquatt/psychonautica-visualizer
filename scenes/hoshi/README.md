# HOSHI 88 (`hoshi`)

A superflat smiling cosmos. Original kawaii space characters (ringed planets, chubby layered stars, ray-crowned suns, rainbow-tailed comets, porthole rockets) with crisp ink outlines and glossy eyes, arranged as a tilted solar system, a spiral galaxy, or a parade marching toward a giant sun on the horizon.

## How it moves with the music
- Beat: planets step one slot along their orbits per beat; alternate orbits or rows move opposite ways.
- Kick: pumps the planets and the sun and fires the rocket flames.
- Snare: a wave of colour sweeps outward from the sun, every character stepping one palette colour.
- Vocals and leads: the characters sing. The big characters and the sun open their mouths on vocal syllables, and the vocal range lights the conveyor belts and planet rings.
- Spectrum: each orbit or row sings one of the eight bands (the innermost is the sub); the chord range swells its own orbits.
- Hats and highs: keep a field of sparkles alive and twinkle the stars.
- Builds: the orbits spin faster.
- Drops: a big bang. A new cosmos bursts out of the centre, everything spins a full turn, and often a flyby or a giant planet arrives.

## Framings (Auto Show)
- far (`0.3`): giant smiling ringed planets made of hundreds of characters float over the system.
- home (`0.6`): the whole system with its orbits and its sun.
- close (`1.05`): the sun and the nearest characters large in frame.
- kaleidoscope: folds in about 20% of visits; best fold 12; acceptable 16 and 1 (the left-right mirror).

## Cost
Medium (`cost: 1.5`). A range of fine printed detail on the characters is Native quality and above (`#if HD>=1`); the base tier never sees it.

## Known weak points
- Very close up, the largest characters are bold flat layers with only subtle added detail.
- Comets, saucers and contrails have less ornament than the planets and stars.
- The sub-bass is the weakest input.
- Not tested on phones.
